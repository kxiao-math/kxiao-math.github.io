Annecy (France) 31/12/2024

Here are short introductions of my works.

Sobolev maps to manifolds. In general, when considering maps that are constrained to take their values into manifolds, smooth manifold-valued maps need not be dense in the corresponding Sobolev spaces of manifold-valued maps. This raises two natural questions: (Q1) when does the strong density property holds; and (Q2) if the strong density fails, how to characterize maps that can be approximated with smooth maps. Other related directions of research that have received considerable attention include notably the weak density property, the extension property, and the lifting property. My works focuses on the low regularity setting both from the point of view of the maps and their domain, namely fractional Sobolev maps on Lipschitz manifolds. This requires working with nonlocal quantities at a regularity level where even their existence is not clear.

  • My first work, with Antoine Detaille and Petru Mironescu, focused on (Q2) for low regularity Sobolev maps. We proved that, when the target manifold is sphere-like, a Sobolev map can be strongly approximated with smooth maps if and only if its distributional Jacobian vanishes. This generalizes a result announced by Bethuel, Coron, Demengel, and Hélein in 1991. In the process, we have also studied other related topics of independent interest, including: the Brezis–Nirenberg theory of homotopical invariants for VMO maps on metric measure spaces, integral invariants for VMO maps on Lipschitz manifolds via homotopy, and some properties of distributional pullbacks by fractional Sobolev maps.
  • My second work, with Antoine Detaille, focused on pullback of forms and lifting properties in a low regularity setting. The idea of this work is to see whether lifting and the topology of covering spaces can be used in order to lower the previously known regularity thresholds on maps allowing to define the pullback of forms. We give a robust meaning to the pullback in the sense of distributions, providing a lifting procedure to “kill” the cohomology class of the form whose pullback is taken. On the way, we establish several results of independent interest regarding low regularity mappings defined on Lipschitz manifolds: the strong density of "almost smooth" maps as well as existence, (essential) uniqueness, and selection principles for liftings of Sobolev and vanishing mean oscillation maps.

Regularity of minimizers of anisotropic interaction functionals. I study minimization problems for nonlocal interaction energies induced by anisotropic Coulomb-type kernels. Basic questions for such problems include existence and uniqueness of minimizers, as well as the Haudorff dimension of their support, their regularity, and in special cases their complete characterization. Recently, it has been proved that for quadratic conﬁnement and under the assumption that the Fourier transform of the kernel is strictly positive, the minimizer is a normalized characteristic function of an ellipsoid.

  • With Tristan Bullion-Gauthier, we focused on the regularity of minimizers in the case of anisotropic kernels and general confinement potentials. We proved that, under certain natural assumptions on the kernel and its Fourier transform, the minimizer has L2 density. For this purpose, we associated with the minimization of functionals certain nonlocal variational inequalities, involving linear and bilinear forms defined in terms of the Fourier transform, and obtained the regularity of minimizers by studying nonlocal penalized problems.
